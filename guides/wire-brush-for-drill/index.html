Wire brushes for drills: end brushes, wheel brushes and cup brushes

A shank-mounted wire brush fits a drill chuck and reaches places an angle grinder cannot. The three shapes do different jobs.

ShapeWorks onTypical job
End brushThe tipHoles, inside pipe, corners, weld roots
Wheel brushThe edgeEdges, grooves, threads, narrow slots
Cup brushThe faceSmall flat areas, brackets, railings

All three are in our shank-mounted wire brushes for drills. End brushes run from 10 to 30 mm head diameter.

What to specify

  • Shank diameter: it must fit the chuck; state it in the order.
  • Head diameter and shape.
  • Wire style: crimped for general cleaning, knotted for heavy rust and welds.
  • Wire material: carbon steel, copper-coated steel, stainless steel or brass.

If the part must not be scratched

Use a shank-mounted abrasive nylon brush instead: the listed shank-mounted models have a 6 mm shank and come in grit 80 (60/120 on request).

For resellers

Drill brushes sell well as sets. We pack them on blister cards with the models and pieces per card you choose.

Safety

The maximum RPM marked on the brush must be equal to or higher than the machine speed. Use the guard, eye protection and gloves, and ask Frank to confirm the RPM of the model you choose.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a wire brush in a drill?

Yes, with a shank-mounted brush that fits the chuck. Respect the maximum RPM of the brush.

Which drill brush for holes?

An end brush: it works on its tip and reaches holes, pipe interiors and corners.

What shank do drill wire brushes have?

It depends on the model; state the chuck size and Frank confirms the shank.
