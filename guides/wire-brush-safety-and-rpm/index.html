Wire brush safety: RPM, wire loss and proper use

A power wire brush is safe when it is matched to the machine and used with light pressure. Most problems come from too much speed or too much force.

1. Never exceed the marked RPM

The maximum RPM on the brush must be equal to or higher than the no-load speed of the machine. A large brush on a small, fast grinder is the most common mistake.

2. Guard and protection

Keep the guard fitted. Wear safety glasses or a face shield, gloves and long sleeves: loose wires travel far. Keep other people away from the work area.

3. Run it in

Before first use, run a new brush at working speed for about a minute inside the guard, pointed away from people. Loose wires leave at this stage.

4. Why wires break off

  • Too much pressure: the wire bends and fatigues. Let the tips do the work.
  • Wrong angle: a cup brush should work with its face, a wheel with its edge.
  • Too much speed for the brush diameter.
  • A worn or damaged brush: replace it.

5. Mount it correctly

The thread must match the spindle exactly, including the pitch (M10 × 1.5 and M10 × 1.25 are different). On bench grinders use the right bushing and set the tool rest close to the wheel.

Frequently asked questions

What RPM should a wire brush run at?

Never above the maximum RPM marked on the brush. The machine speed must be equal or lower.

Why do wires fly off a wire brush?

Too much pressure, the wrong angle, too much speed or a worn brush.

What protection is needed?

The machine guard, safety glasses or a face shield, gloves and long sleeves.
