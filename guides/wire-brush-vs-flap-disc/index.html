Wire brush vs flap disc: which one should you use?

Both go on an angle grinder and both are used on metal, but they do opposite jobs. A wire brush cleans what sits on the surface. A flap disc removes the surface itself.

Wire brushFlap disc
Removes base metalNoYes
Best forRust, scale, paint, slag, spatterGrinding, blending welds, deburring, shaping
FinishCleaned, original profile keptGround, smooth, dimension changed
Uneven surfacesFollows themFlattens them

Choose a wire brush when

You want to keep the part as it is and only clean it: before painting, between weld passes, on threads, castings or textured surfaces. See knotted cup brushes and crimped cup brushes.

Choose a flap disc when

You need to take material off: level a weld, remove a burr, round an edge or prepare a bevel. See abrasive flap discs; specify diameter, bore, grit and backing.

Often you need both

A common sequence on welded work is: wire brush to remove slag, flap disc to blend the weld, then a brush or abrasive nylon for the final clean before paint. For resellers, the two sell together.

Frequently asked questions

Does a wire brush remove metal?

No. It removes rust, scale and paint and leaves the base metal. A flap disc removes metal.

Which is better for removing rust?

A wire brush if you only need to clean; a flap disc if the surface must also be ground smooth.

Can I blend a weld with a wire brush?

No. Use a flap disc to blend; use the wire brush to remove slag and spatter.
