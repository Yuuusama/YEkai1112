Wire brushes for wood texturing and distressing

Brushing wood removes the soft early grain and leaves the hard grain standing, which gives the textured, aged look used in furniture, beams and flooring. It is usually done in two steps.

Step 1: open the grain with wire

A crimped steel wire brush cuts the soft grain quickly. Work along the grain, with light pressure, and test on an offcut first. Use a crimped wire cup brush on an angle grinder for boards, or a wide-face crimped wheel on a bench machine for small parts. Knotted brushes are usually too aggressive for wood.

Step 2: clean and finish with abrasive nylon

An abrasive nylon brush removes the fibres the wire leaves behind and smooths the surface without flattening the texture. Grit 80 is the most ordered; 120 gives a slightly finer finish. Grit 60 and 120 are made on request.

Which to choose

GoalBrush
Deep texture on softwoodCrimped steel wire, then nylon grit 80
Light texture, hardwoodAbrasive nylon grit 80 only
Cleaning before stain or oilAbrasive nylon grit 120 or finer

Shapes for furniture workshops

Abrasive nylon comes as wheel (100 to 150 mm), cup (65 to 125 mm) and shank-mounted models for drills. Colour alone does not identify the grit; confirm it for each model. More in abrasive nylon brush: uses and grit.

Ask for samples

Wood species react differently, so test before a bulk order. Tell Frank the wood, the machine and the look you want, and ask for sample brushes in two grits.

Frequently asked questions

What brush is used to texture wood?

A crimped steel wire brush to open the grain, followed by an abrasive nylon brush to clean and finish.

What grit of nylon brush for wood?

Grit 80 is the most ordered; 120 gives a slightly finer finish.

Should I brush with or against the grain?

Along the grain, with light pressure. Test on an offcut first.
