How to identify a good quality wire brush

Two wire brushes can look the same in a photo and perform very differently. These are the points to check on a sample before a bulk order.

1. Wire material and diameter

Ask what the wire is: carbon steel, copper-coated steel, stainless 304 or 201, or brass. Measure the wire diameter on the sample and check that it matches the quote.

2. Fill density

More wire means longer life and a more even action. Compare the weight of samples of the same size: a much lighter brush has less wire.

3. Knots and trim

On knotted brushes the knots should be tight and even, with the same length all round. On crimped brushes the face should be even, with no long stray wires.

4. Balance

An unbalanced brush vibrates, wears the grinder and tires the operator. At YEKAI we check brushes on a balancing tester and sample every production batch.

5. Wire retention

Run the sample at working speed inside a guard. Every brush sheds a few wires at first; a good one stops shedding quickly.

6. Mount and marking

The thread or arbor hole must be clean and true, and the brush should be marked with its maximum RPM.

Ask the factory

  • Samples from normal production, not specially made
  • The wire material and diameter in writing
  • The maximum RPM of each model
  • How batches are inspected

Frequently asked questions

How do I check wire brush quality?

Check the wire material and diameter, fill density, knot tightness, balance, wire retention and RPM marking on a sample.

Why does a wire brush vibrate?

Usually because it is unbalanced. Balanced brushes run smoother and last longer.

Is it normal for a new wire brush to lose wires?

A few at first, yes. A good brush stops shedding quickly; run it in inside the guard.
