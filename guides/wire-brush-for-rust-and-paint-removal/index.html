Wire brush for rust and paint removal: how to choose

A wire brush removes rust, old paint and dirt without eating into the metal, which is why it is used before painting or welding. The choice depends on how stubborn the coating is and on the shape of the part.

By how heavy the job is

SituationWire style
Surface rust, loose paint, general cleaningCrimped: more flexible, more even finish
Thick rust, scale, firmly bonded paintKnotted: stiffer and more aggressive
Parts that must not be scratched, fine finishAbrasive nylon instead of wire

The full difference is in knotted vs crimped wire brush.

By the shape of the part

Large flat surfaces (sheet, gates, structures): a cup brush on the angle grinder. Crimped: crimped wire cup brush, 2.5 to 6 in. Knotted: knotted wire cup brush.

Loose parts on a bench grinder (tools, fasteners, components): wide-face crimped wire wheel, 4 to 12 in, or copper-coated wire wheel.

Corners and edges: knotted bevel brush.

Holes, railings and small areas: shank-mounted wire brushes for drills (end, wheel and cup shapes).

Hand touch-ups: hand wire brush.

When to use abrasive nylon or a flap disc

If the part must not be scratched, an abrasive nylon brush removes rust, corrosion, paint and primer with less marking. If you also need to remove material or blend a weld, that is a job for a flap disc, not a wire brush.

For resellers: what to stock first

Rust and paint jobs are covered by a few items: a crimped cup and a knotted cup for angle grinders, a wheel brush for bench grinders and a shank-mounted set for drills. They can be supplied on blister cards for peg displays.

Safety

The maximum safe speed (RPM) marked on the brush must be equal to or higher than the speed of the machine. Always use the guard, eye protection and gloves. Ask Frank to confirm the RPM of the model you choose.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best wire brush for removing rust?

Light rust: crimped. Thick rust or scale: knotted. On flat surfaces use a cup brush on an angle grinder.

Does a wire brush scratch metal?

It leaves marks, knotted more than crimped. If the part must not be scratched, use an abrasive nylon brush.

Which wire brush do I use with a drill?

A shank-mounted brush: an end brush for holes, a wheel for edges or a cup for open surfaces.
