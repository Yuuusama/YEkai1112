Wire brush for weld cleaning: which type for each job

Weld cleaning is almost always done with a knotted (twist knot) wire brush: it is stiffer than a crimped brush and removes slag, spatter and scale without grinding the metal away. What changes from job to job is the shape.

Weld bead and root pass: stringer bead wheel

The stringer bead knot wheel has narrow knots that follow the bead and enter tight bevels. Our series is carbon steel, typical wire 0.5 mm, in 4, 4.5, 5, 6, 7 and 8 in. To quote, give the working width and the arbor hole.

Plate and wide surfaces: knotted cup brush

To clean around the weld on sheet, plate or sections, a cup brush works on its face and covers more area: knotted wire cup brush or banded knot cup brush, which keeps the knots tighter.

Corners and fillet welds: bevel brush

The knotted bevel brush has angled knots and reaches fillets, corners and edges where a cup does not fit. Series in 4, 4.5 and 5 in, typical wire 0.5 mm.

Holes, pipe and small areas: end brush for drills

Where the angle grinder does not fit, use a shank-mounted brush. End brushes run from 10 to 30 mm head diameter.

Hand touch-up

Between weld passes or for a quick touch-up, a hand wire brush does the job.

Safety

The maximum safe speed (RPM) marked on the brush must be equal to or higher than the speed of the machine. Always use the guard, eye protection and gloves. Ask Frank to confirm the RPM of the model you choose.

Frequently asked questions

What wire brush is used to clean welds?

A knotted wire brush. A stringer bead wheel for the bead, a knotted cup for the surrounding surface and a bevel brush for corners.

Can a crimped wire brush clean welds?

For light cleaning, yes, but for slag and spatter a knotted brush works better because it is stiffer.

What sizes do stringer bead wheels come in?

In our series: 4, 4.5, 5, 6, 7 and 8 in, typical wire 0.5 mm carbon steel.
