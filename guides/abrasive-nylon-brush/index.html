Abrasive nylon brush: what it is for and which grit to choose

An abrasive nylon brush (abrasive filament brush) has nylon bristles with abrasive grain mixed through the whole filament. As it wears it exposes fresh grain, so the finish stays consistent.

What it is used for

Our catalog lists it for removing rust, corrosion, paint and primer and for surface cleaning. Compared with a wire brush it marks the part less and leaves no steel residue. It is also used on wood to clean and raise the grain; in that case confirm the grit with a sample.

Shapes and sizes in the catalog

  • Wheel: 100 to 150 mm
  • Cup: 65 to 125 mm
  • Shank-mounted wheel: 50 to 100 mm
  • Shank-mounted cup: 50 to 100 mm

The listed shank-mounted models have a 6 mm shank, for drills. Arbor holes and threads on cups are confirmed with Frank. See all models in abrasive nylon filament brushes.

Which grit to choose

The catalog covers grit 80 (60/120 on request). Lower number: more aggressive, faster removal. Higher number: finer finish. Grit 80 is the most ordered for general cleaning and for wood; 120 leaves a slightly finer finish.

Aluminium oxide (AO) or silicon carbide (SiC)

The catalog offers both depending on the model. Note: brush colour alone does not identify the abrasive or the grit; always ask for it in writing for the model you choose.

What to send for a quote

  • Part material (steel, aluminium, wood…) and the finish you want
  • Machine: angle grinder, drill, bench grinder
  • Shape and diameter
  • Grit and abrasive, if already defined
  • Quantity per model

Frequently asked questions

What is an abrasive nylon brush used for?

For removing rust, corrosion, paint and primer and cleaning surfaces, with less marking than a wire brush.

Which grit should I choose?

Standard is grit 80 with 1.3 mm filament; 60 or 120 is made on request. Lower is more aggressive; higher gives a finer finish.

Does the colour show the grit?

No. Colour alone does not identify the abrasive or the grit; confirm it for each model.
